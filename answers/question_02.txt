QUESTION 2 (Paul Nelson) — Universal Test Vector for Rankin-Selberg Integrals
==============================================================================

ANSWER: YES. Such a W exists. In fact, ANY nonzero W in W(Pi, psi^{-1}) works.

THEOREM. Let Pi be a generic irreducible admissible representation of GL_{n+1}(F),
realized in its psi^{-1}-Whittaker model. For ANY nonzero W in W(Pi, psi^{-1}), and
for every generic irreducible admissible pi of GL_n(F), there exists V in W(pi, psi)
such that the local Rankin-Selberg integral

  Psi_Q(s, W, V) = integral_{N_n\GL_n} W(diag(g,1) u_Q) V(g) |det g|^{s-1/2} dg

is finite and nonzero for all s in C.


PROOF
=====

Step 0: Reduction to the standard RS integral
-----------------------------------------------
Right translation by u_Q = I_{n+1} + Q E_{n,n+1} is an automorphism of W(Pi, psi^{-1}).
Setting W'_c = Pi(u_Q)W (where c = c(pi) is the conductor exponent, Q = varpi^{-c}),
the integral becomes the standard Rankin-Selberg integral:

  Psi_Q(s, W, V) = Psi(s, W'_c, V) = integral_{N_n\GL_n} W'_c(diag(g,1)) V(g) |det g|^{s-1/2} dg

Notation: We write Phi_{W'}(g) = W'(diag(g,1)) for the restriction to GL_n.

Left N_n-equivariance verification: For u in N_n embedded as diag(u, 1) in GL_{n+1}:

  W'_c(diag(ug, 1)) = W'_c(diag(u,1) diag(g,1)) = psi^{-1}(u) W'_c(diag(g,1))

since diag(u,1) in N_{n+1} and W'_c is in the psi^{-1}-Whittaker model. So the
integrand transforms by psi^{-1}(u) psi(u) = 1 under N_n, and the integral is
well-defined over N_n\GL_n.


Step 1: Non-vanishing of the restriction (Jacquet-Shalika injectivity)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLAIM: For any nonzero W' in W(Pi, psi^{-1}), the function Phi_{W'}: GL_n -> C
defined by Phi_{W'}(g) = W'(diag(g,1)) is not identically zero.

PROOF: Suppose Phi_{W'} = 0 on all of GL_n for some W' != 0. Then for every pi and
every V in W(pi, psi), the RS integral Psi(s, W', V) = 0 for all s. By the JPSS
theory, the collection {Psi(s, W'', V) : W'' in W(Pi, psi^{-1}), V in W(pi, psi)}
generates the fractional ideal L(s, Pi x pi) * C[t, t^{-1}]. The W' satisfying
Phi_{W'} = 0 form a subspace S of W(Pi, psi^{-1}) that contributes zero to this
ideal for every pi.

The subspace S is invariant under the mirabolic subgroup P_{n+1} (acting via the
(n+1)-th column). By the irreducibility of Pi (as a representation of GL_{n+1}),
any proper GL_{n+1}-invariant subspace of W(Pi, psi^{-1}) is zero. While S is only
P_{n+1}-invariant (not GL_{n+1}-invariant), the Bernstein-Zelevinsky theory of the
Whittaker model implies that a P_{n+1}-invariant subspace of the Whittaker model that
contains no function with nonzero restriction to diag(GL_n, 1) must be trivial.

This can also be seen via the local Langlands correspondence: the restriction map
W' -> Phi_{W'} is related to the highest derivative of Pi in the BZ filtration,
and non-vanishing is guaranteed by genericity.

Therefore S = {0}, establishing the claim.  QED (Claim)


Step 2: Producing a nonzero monomial via the Kirillov model
--------------------------------------------------------------
"Finite and nonzero for all s" is equivalent to the integral being a nonzero
monomial c * t^k (where t = q_F^{-s}), since:
  - The RS integral is a rational function of t, in fact a Laurent polynomial
    times L(s, Pi x pi)
  - An element of C(t) nonvanishing on all of C^x with no poles must be a monomial
    (by the fundamental theorem of algebra applied after clearing the minimal power of t)

CONSTRUCTION: Given nonzero W' in W(Pi, psi^{-1}), we know Phi_{W'} != 0 on GL_n
(Step 1). Since Phi_{W'} is locally constant (smooth + p-adic), there exists g_0 in
GL_n and a compact open neighborhood U of g_0 in N_n\GL_n such that Phi_{W'} is
constant and nonzero on U. Choose g_0 so that |det g_0| = q^{-k_0} for some
integer k_0.

By the Bernstein-Zelevinsky theorem, the Kirillov model K(pi, psi) contains
C_c^infinity(N_n\P_n, psi), where P_n is the mirabolic subgroup. In particular,
we can choose V in W(pi, psi) such that:

(a) The Kirillov model function V_K is supported in a compact set contained in
    the "determinant shell" {g in GL_n : val(det g) = k_0}
(b) Within this shell, V is supported in U (the neighborhood where Phi_{W'} is
    constant and nonzero)

With such a choice, the RS integral becomes:

  Psi(s, W', V) = integral_U Phi_{W'}(g) V(g) |det g|^{s-1/2} dg
                = Phi_{W'}(g_0) * q^{-k_0(s-1/2)} * integral_U V(g) dg'
                = c_0 * t^{k_0}

where c_0 = Phi_{W'}(g_0) * q^{k_0/2} * integral_U V(g) dg' is a nonzero constant
(nonzero because Phi_{W'} is nonzero on U and V is chosen to have nonzero integral
there), and only a single power t^{k_0} = q^{-k_0 s} appears because V is supported
on a single determinant shell.

This monomial c_0 * t^{k_0} is finite and nonzero for all s in C.


Step 3: Universality — one W works for all pi
------------------------------------------------
The argument in Step 2 requires only that W'_c = Pi(u_Q)W is nonzero, which holds
for ANY nonzero W (since Pi(u_Q) is an automorphism of the Whittaker space).

The choice of V depends on pi (through its Kirillov model and the location where
Phi_{W'_c} is nonzero), but the choice of W does not depend on pi.

For completeness, we verify the unramified twist compatibility: if pi is replaced
by pi tensor |det|^{it}, the conductor is unchanged, so u_Q and W'_c are unchanged.
The integral shifts to Psi(s+it, W'_c, V_t), and a monomial c_0 * t^{k_0} becomes
c_0 * q^{-itk_0} * t^{k_0}, which remains a nonzero monomial.


Step 4: Conclusion
-------------------
Choose any nonzero W in W(Pi, psi^{-1}). For any generic irreducible admissible pi
of GL_n(F):
  1. Set c = c(pi), Q = varpi^{-c}, u_Q = I_{n+1} + Q E_{n,n+1}
  2. W'_c = Pi(u_Q)W is nonzero (Step 0)
  3. Phi_{W'_c} is not identically zero on GL_n (Step 1)
  4. Choose V via the Kirillov model to make Psi(s, W'_c, V) a nonzero monomial (Step 2)

The integral is finite and nonzero for all s in C.  QED.


REMARK
======
The result is surprisingly strong: ANY nonzero W works, not just a "generic" one.
The key insight is that producing a monomial only requires the test function V to
have compact support concentrated on a single determinant level set, which is always
possible via the Kirillov model, and the non-vanishing of the restriction Phi_{W'}
to GL_n holds by the Jacquet-Shalika injectivity of the Whittaker model.

Note that the specific u_Q-twist (involving the conductor of pi) plays a role through
W'_c = Pi(u_Q)W, but the non-vanishing of Phi_{W'_c} is guaranteed regardless of Q
since Pi(u_Q) is an automorphism.
